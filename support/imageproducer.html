Image Producer

A layered image editor for Mac and iPad. Draw, cut out, key out a color, add a glow, generate with Image Playground, and make stop-motion animation.

Getting started

  1. New Image makes a new picture and saves it to iCloud Drive with a number for a name, like ImageProducer0024. On the iPad, Image Producer asks for a name and a size first. On the Mac, press ⌘N.
  2. New from Import… starts a new picture from a photo or a PDF. The photo or PDF itself is never changed. On the Mac, press ⇧⌘N.
  3. Image Producer saves your work by itself a moment after you stop.
One rule runs the whole app: a tool works on the layer selected in the Layers list. Select the layer first, then use the tool. Each tool’s panel names the layer the tool will change.
There is no ⌘Z. Most tools put their result on a new layer and hide the original underneath it. To go back, delete the new layer and turn the original back on. The History tab can also take you back to any earlier step.

The window

The tool panel and Layers share the right half. Tapping or clicking one widens it.
  1. Canvas — the left half of the window.
  2. Tool box — two rows of tools under the canvas. The chosen tool is tinted; on the Mac, hover one to see its name.
  3. Tool panel — the settings for the chosen tool. The top of the panel names the tool and the selected layer.
  4. Layers / History — a two-way switch at the top of the right column.
  5. Status line — the thin line along the bottom. After every action the status line says what just happened.
  6. Zoom controls — full-screen canvas, fit width, zoom in, the zoom level (tap or click to return to 100%), zoom out.
  7. Floating preview — a small copy of your picture at real size, whatever the zoom. Drag the preview to any corner.
  8. Window toolbar (top right): Export, Share, Save to Desktop (Mac), and About Image Producer.

Saving and going back

  • Image Producer saves on its own. The status line says “Autosaving…” and then “Autosaved”.
  • On the Mac, ⌘S also saves and marks the spot, so File ▸ Revert to Last Save… can return there.
  • Opening a picture marks that spot too, so File ▸ Revert to Open… can return there.
Revert to Open and Revert to Last Save throw away everything after that spot, including History. Both spots are forgotten when you quit.

History

  • Open the History tab and tap or click a step to look at it. Looking changes nothing.
  • To go back to that step for good, press Keep this step at the top of History. The newer steps are deleted, and Image Producer asks first.
  • Long-press (iPad) or right-click (Mac) a step for Restore from This Step or Delete This Step.
  • The ⋯ menu has Optimize History, which makes the file smaller and keeps every step, and Purge History…, which keeps the picture and erases the steps.
If History grows past 250 MB, Image Producer warns you. Try Optimize History first.

Layers

  • Each layer has a checkbox, a name and an eye. The checkbox is for merging and linking, not for selecting. A green eye means the layer shows; a red crossed-out eye means the layer is hidden.
  • Tap or click a layer’s name to select the layer. Drag to change the order. On the iPad, drag by the handle at the right.
  • + adds a new layer above the selected one.
  • Long-press (iPad) or right-click (Mac) a layer for Rename, Duplicate and Delete.
  • Merge: check two or more layers and press Merge. The layers become one picture on a new layer, and the originals are kept, hidden.
  • Link: check two or more layers and press Link. Moving or resizing one linked layer moves them all, and each layer stays editable.
Light and Dark are the bottom two layers. They become the light and dark versions when you export an Icon Set.

The tools

Every picture opens on the Canvas tool. Tap or click ⓘ in a panel for more about that setting.

Canvas

The picture’s name, size, print settings and exports.

  • Project name renames the file. Type the new name and press Return.
  • Resolution: for printing, set the resolution first — 300 is standard for print.
  • Size: type the width and height, or choose from Canvas size presets. The presets are grouped by job: App Store Connect, Wallpapers, Social, Apple TV, Web ads, Paper, Posters, Signs & flyers, Event & stationery, Cards & envelopes, and Photo. Each preset shows its size and whether it is horizontal, vertical or square.
  • Size rule decides what happens when you change the width or height:
    • Exact — type any width and height.
    • Fixed ratio — the shape stays the same; change one side and the other follows.
    • Fluid width — the height stays put and the width can grow or shrink. Fluid height is the reverse. The picture keeps its size and stays centered; only the canvas around it changes.
  • Landscape swaps the width and the height. A square canvas does not change.
  • Print setup: bleed, safe margin, crop marks, registration marks, and RGB or CMYK. Each one has an ⓘ that explains it.
  • Export: Print PDF, a web folder of PNGs, every app-icon size, or Single image… for one PNG, JPEG, TIFF or PDF.
The first time you rename a numbered picture, the file is renamed. After that, each rename saves a copy under the new name and keeps the old file.
A print PDF over 150 MB shows a warning, because some print shops will not take a bigger file. The PDF is still saved.

Color Palette

Every color in the app comes from the palette. Tap or click a swatch to use the color, and change the color with the picker. Add color and Remove grow and shrink the palette. Save Palette and Load Palette move a palette between pictures.

Move / Transform

  1. Select the layer.
  2. Drag inside the dashed box to move the layer. Drag a corner to resize the layer.
  3. Or use the Scale and Rotation sliders. Fit puts the picture inside the canvas; Fill covers the whole canvas.
  4. Choose another tool when you are done. The original is kept underneath, hidden.

Crop is here too: choose a shape, adjust the box, then press Apply Crop.

Mask

The mask marks the part of the picture to keep. Everything outside the mask goes dim, and exports keep only what is inside. The mask can be a rectangle, an oval, a polygon, a star, a typed character or a symbol. The mask never changes your picture.

  1. Press Add Mask.
  2. Drag a corner to resize the mask. Drag inside to move the mask.
  3. Remove Mask takes the mask off.

Paint Drop / Bucket

  • Fill Layer fills the whole selected layer with the color.
  • On a picture, move over an area to see what will fill, then tap or click inside an outlined area to pour the color up to the outline. Tolerance sets how strict the outline is.
Fill Layer fills the entire layer. To change the color of a symbol, use the Symbol tool instead.

Pen (Pixels)

The Pen draws in squares, like graph paper. Each layer has its own grid size. Choose a color and drag on the canvas. Switch to Erase to take squares away. On the Mac, dragging with a right-click also erases.

Eraser

  • Brush — drag to erase by hand.
  • Magic — pick the background color with the Eyedropper, raise Tolerance until the whole background turns magenta, then press Erase Color → Transparent.
Fill a layer with black behind the picture before you erase. Leftover bits are much easier to see against black.

Eyedropper

Tap or click the picture to pick up a color. The color goes into the palette and becomes the color for filling and drawing. A bigger Sample size averages more of the picture, for a steadier color on a soft background.

Text

  1. Type in the Text field.
  2. Press New Text Layer. The new layer is named after its words.
  3. Choose a font, a size, a color, and bold, italic or underline. Every change shows right away.
  4. Press Return to finish a line, type the next line, and press New Text Layer again.
  5. Use Move to place the text.

Renaming a text layer changes the words on the canvas. Emoji are the exception: an emoji layer is named with the emoji’s name, like “Sunflower” for 🌻, and renaming the layer never changes the emoji.

Outline draws an edge around the letters in any palette color. White letters with a thin black outline read well on a busy picture.

Symbol

  1. Pick a Color.
  2. Search for a symbol, or tap or click one from the starting set. Nothing changes on the canvas yet — a preview in your color shows above the search box.
  3. Press Create New Layer to add the symbol on a new layer, or Apply to Selected Layer to swap the symbol on the selected symbol layer.
To recolor or replace a symbol you already placed, select the symbol’s layer, pick a color, tap the same symbol or a different one, and press Apply to Selected Layer. The symbol keeps its size and place.
SF Symbols belong to Apple. Apple does not allow them, or anything that looks like them, in an app icon or a logo. They are fine in your own pictures.

Image

Import Image to New Layer… adds a picture on a new layer named for the file. Import Image to Selected Layer… replaces what is on the selected layer. Use Move to size and place the picture. Image Producer opens PNG, JPEG, HEIC, TIFF, GIF and BMP, and on the Mac, PSD.

The Image Playground Tool

The Image Playground Tool hands your words to Apple’s Image Playground, Apple’s image generation on your own device. Turn on Apple Intelligence in Settings first. The Image Playground Tool works two ways:

Add a new layer

  1. Type a few key words in the Image Playground Tool’s box that describe what Apple’s Image Playground should make — not in Apple’s window.
  2. Press Image Playground: New Layer.
  3. Apple’s Image Playground opens with your key words. Make an image, choose a style if you like, and press Done.

The picture is added on a new layer, named with your key words.

Restyle the selected layer

  1. Select a layer with a picture.
  2. Press Filter → Edit Current Layer, choose a style in Apple’s Image Playground, and press Done.

The restyled picture goes on a new layer above, and the original is kept underneath, hidden.

Layer… — Translucent, Glow, Green Key, Gradient, Blend

Five effects for the selected layer. You see each effect as you adjust it, and nothing is permanent until you press Apply. Apply keeps the original underneath, hidden.

  • Translucent — makes the layer see-through.
  • Glow — a soft glow around the picture, in one color (Neon) or two (Plasma).
  • Green Key — makes one color disappear everywhere on the layer, like a green screen.
  • Gradient — on an empty layer, a blend of two colors, with Blend starts and Blend ends to make the blend wide and soft or short and sharp; on a picture, a fade.
  • Blend — mixes the layer with the layers below: darker, lighter, or more contrast. Strength sets how much.

Blend: light on black, over a photo

Green Key on a black background

  1. Select the layer. Open Green Key and check Key this layer.
  2. Pick black as the color to remove.
  3. Keep Tolerance low, so only the background goes.
  4. Raise Softness until soft light fades into the layer below instead of ending in a ragged edge.
  5. Press Apply.
Too much Softness also fades dark parts of your subject. Lower Softness to keep the subject solid.
Glow needs an edge to glow from. A layer that fills the whole canvas, like Light or Dark, has no edge — select the layer with your picture.

Remove Background

Select a layer with a picture and press Remove Background. Image Producer finds the subject and clears everything behind it. If Image Producer finds more than one subject, choose the ones to keep. The cutout goes on a new layer, and the original is kept underneath, hidden.

Remove Background is not perfect. On the lighthouse, a wisp of cloud stayed attached to the tower. Finish with the Eraser.

Magic Lasso

Move over an area to see it outlined in red, then tap or click to clear it — sky, then water, then rock. Only the connected area clears. All the clears go on one new layer, and History steps back through them one at a time.

Zoom

Double-click to zoom in. On the Mac, Option-double-click zooms out and Shift-Option-double-click returns to 100%. On the iPad, pinch. Drag to move around. The Zoom panel turns the floating preview on and off.

Camera

Press Capture to take a picture of everything you can see, onto a new top layer. The capture is also copied, ready to paste anywhere. You can choose a see-through or solid background, a larger size, and trimming to the picture.

Each capture replaces whatever you had copied.

Stop motion and the Lightbox

The Camera also makes stop-motion animation. Each capture is one frame.

  1. Put each moving part on its own layer.
  2. Choose Camera and turn on Animation mode.
  3. Press Capture for frame 1.
  4. Switch to Move and nudge the moving part a little. Switch back to Camera and press Capture again. Repeat.
  5. Turn on Onion skin to see the last few frames faintly while you set up the next one.
  6. Press Play to watch. Set the speed, and leave Loop on to watch it repeat. Exit preview goes back to editing.
  7. To smooth the motion, set Frames between and press Generate. Image Producer draws the in-between frames for you.
In Animation mode, a move you did not capture is not kept. Pressing Capture is what saves each position.
Slow in + out makes motion start and stop gently, like real things do.

Projects to try

Make an animated GIF

A star that slides across the picture and loops.

  1. Make a New Image and select Foreground.
  2. Choose Symbol, search “star”, tap or click a star, and press Create New Layer.
  3. Choose Camera, turn on Animation mode, and press Capture.
  4. Choose Move and drag the star a little to the right. Choose Camera and press Capture.
  5. Repeat until you have five or six frames. Press Play to check, then Exit preview.
  6. Press Export and choose Animated GIF. Leave Camera frames only on, and press Export….
GIFs have a limited number of colors, so soft glows can look rough. Turning on a solid background when you capture helps.

Make a banner ad

A 1500 × 500 banner: a 3D headline, a picture, a symbol on each side, and a tagline.

Before using any tool that adds a layer, select the layer the new one should sit above.
  1. Make a New Image. Choose Canvas, open Canvas size presets ▸ Social, and pick X header — 1500 × 500.
  2. Select Background. Choose Paint Drop / Bucket, pick a color, and press Fill Layer.
  3. Choose Text, type “Image Producer”, pick white, and press New Text Layer. Choose a font and a size.
  4. Duplicate the headline twice. Give the copies darker colors and nudge each one a few pixels with Move. The stacked copies look 3D.
  5. Check all three headline layers and press Link, so the three move as one.
  6. Select Foreground. Choose Image and press Import Image to New Layer…. The picture lands behind the headline.
  7. Choose Text, type the tagline, and press New Text Layer. With Move, drag a side corner of the text box outward until the tagline fits on one line, and drag the tagline to the bottom.
  8. Add a symbol on each side with Symbol ▸ Create New Layer, and size them with Move.
  9. Save the banner with Save to Desktop (Mac) or Share. The banner comes out 1500 × 500.
For a banner you will publish, use your own artwork on the sides, not SF Symbols — see Symbol.
Link is not Merge. Linked layers stay editable text. Merge turns layers into one flat picture.

Make a three-color stacked picture (iPad)

Four symbols stacked into one picture: three colored sheets with a black outline on top.

  1. Start with a background — this one is a purple-to-blue gradient from Layer… ▸ Gradient. Choose Symbol and search “layers”.
  2. Pick blue, tap 3 Layers 3D Bottom Filled, and press Create New Layer.
  3. Pick green, tap 3 Layers 3D Middle Filled, and press Create New Layer.
  4. Pick red, tap 3 Layers 3D Top Filled, and press Create New Layer.
  5. Pick black, tap the plain 3 Layers 3D outline, and press Create New Layer.
The four symbols are the same shape, so the layers line up exactly with no erasing. To resize the whole picture, link the four layers and resize one with Move.
Because the picture is made from SF Symbols, the picture is for your own use, not for an app icon or a logo.

Cut a subject out of its scene

  1. Import the picture onto its own layer and select the layer.
  2. Choose Remove Background and press the button.
  3. Clean up leftover bits with the Eraser, with black behind the cutout.
  4. Use Move to size and place the cutout.

Remove a black background and keep the light

See Green Key on a black background, under Layer….

Exporting

  • Export saves the picture as PNG, JPEG, TIFF, HEIC, GIF, BMP, PDF, a PDF with one page per layer, an animated GIF, or (Mac) an Icon Set. On the Mac, press ⌘E.
  • Share sends a PNG to Messages, Mail, AirDrop and more.
  • Save to Desktop (Mac) puts a PNG on your Desktop. Save to Desktop never replaces a different file with the same name.
  • About shows the version and contact information.

The saved picture is the size of the canvas — a 1500 × 500 banner comes out 1500 × 500.

Icon Set (Mac) makes a folder with light and dark 1024-pixel PNGs, ready to drag into Xcode.

Troubleshooting

ProblemTry this
A tool does nothingSelect a layer first. The tool’s panel says what kind of layer the tool needs.
Glow or another Layer… effect doesn’t showSelect the layer with your picture, not Light or Dark.
Green Key leaves a ragged edgeRaise Softness.
The subject turns see-through after Green KeyLower Softness, then Tolerance.
A new layer from the Image Playground Tool is just named “Image Playground”Type your key words in the Image Playground Tool’s box before pressing Image Playground: New Layer.
Fill Layer turned a symbol into a solid blockGo back one step in History, then change the color with the Symbol tool.
A History step came back after reopeningLooking at a step does not keep it. Press Keep this step.
I want to undoDelete the new layer and turn the hidden original back on, or use History.
I want to go back furtherFile ▸ Revert to Open… or Revert to Last Save….
